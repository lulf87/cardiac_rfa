# V10 Introduction Gap Statement

## End-of-Introduction Gap / Positioning Paragraphs

Existing computational studies have provided increasingly detailed descriptions of RF lesion formation under standard, HPSD, and very-high-power short-duration conditions. However, the methodological value of reduced electro-thermal models does not lie in reproducing every catheter-specific control feature or chamber-scale flow phenomenon. Their value lies in providing computationally economical, assumption-explicit frameworks for broad comparative sweeps across tissue thickness, cooling, and contact surrogates. For such reduced models, the central question is not whether they mimic a specific device, but whether the comparator definitions, reduction assumptions, and interpretation boundaries are made explicit enough to support defensible comparative inferences.

A key problem arises when a 90 W / 4 s comparator is represented only as a fixed-power pulse and when post-pulse after-heating is omitted. Under those conditions, part of the reported protocol difference may reflect comparator mis-specification and missing thermal latency rather than the intended short high-power protocol behavior itself. In reduced protocol studies, this is not a secondary implementation detail; it is a first-order methodological issue because power titration and post-pulse conduction can materially change lesion depth, delivered energy, and apparent thermal severity.

Accordingly, the aim of the present work is not to reproduce a catheter-specific commercial 90 W / 4 s algorithm. Instead, the objective is to establish a comparator-aware reduced 2D electro-thermal methodology that explicitly distinguishes a fixed-power 90 W / 4 s case from a generic temperature-limited 90 W / 4 s case and that includes a finite post-pulse thermal latency window. The intended outputs are deterministic, assumption-bounded comparisons in lesion depth, depth fraction, delivered energy, and peak-temperature trends across representative wall-thickness, cooling, and insertion-depth conditions.

The main methodological contributions are threefold. First, the framework separates comparator definition from protocol labeling by treating fixed-power and temperature-limited 90 W / 4 s as distinct computational objects. Second, it introduces an explicit post-pulse latency window for short high-power simulations. Third, it uses these additions to generate four-way deterministic comparison and phase-preparation maps while keeping interpretation bounded to reduced-model applicability. Within this framing, lesion width and overheating-related quantities are retained as secondary internal proxies rather than promoted as device-level predictive endpoints.
